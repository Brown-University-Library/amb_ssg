Artist note
Claude Vernet, commonly called Joseph Vernet, was born at Avignon, and taught by his father Antoine Vernet and Adrian Manglard. In 1732 Joseph went to Italy meaning to adopt historical painting, but the charm of the Mediterranean captivated him, and he became a painter of landscape and port scenes. But for a short visit to Greece he remained in Italy for twenty years, most of the time in poverty; he is said to have exchanged his pictures for old suits of clothes. But in time he won his way, and such was his repute by 1750 that Louis V invited him to Paris. The next year he was admitted to the Academie and commissioned to paint a set of about twenty French seaports. After ten years he had done fifteen; wearied by the mechanical nature of his commission he threw it up. He died in Paris 1789. His son was Carl Vernet, his grandson Horace, the well-known battle painter, whose canvases abound at Versailles. Joseph Vernet and Zuccarelli between them established a numerous school of Italian landscapists; the picture described below is of Vernet's School. See P. M. Turner and C. H. Collins Baker: Stories of the French artists. 1909.
Description
In the left foreground two boats with yellow and white sails; on the shore by them a group of men and women. To the right, on a little spit, a man with a long hook over his shoulder and a little boy. Across the bay a town with three square towers conspicuous, on the side of a hill.
