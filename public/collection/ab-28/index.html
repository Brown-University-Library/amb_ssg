Artist note
With barely an exception the Milanese painters capitulated to the Florentine Leonardo da Vinci, and abandoning the old traditions of their school as exemplified by Vincenzo Foppa, one and all essayed to catch the wonderful mystery, the incommunicable spirit of Leonardo's types. The famous Virgin of the Rocks and the yet more famous Mona Lisa are typical of the elusive, haunting meaning that baffled all Leonardo's followers. The purity of his sense of form and this incommunicable mystery became under a Luini and Solario sweetened and mannered; the beautiful became the suavely pretty, the elusive became obvious. Other followers of Leonardo at Milan were Cesare da Cesto, Salaino, Leonardo's favourite pupil, Pedrini, Melzi, Ambrozio di Predis, and Marco d'Oggiono. Solario, in whose manner is the picture described below, is best seen in Milan, London, Berlin, and Paris. Two examples are in the Johnson Collection, Philadelphia.
Description
Head and shoulders, fronting the spectator; the head bent and looking down to the right front. Her hair is deep chestnut, her dress clear scarlet; a bronze brown curtain in the right background.
