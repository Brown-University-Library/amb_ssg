Artist note
Born at Senlis; pupil of Gros and Delaroche; in 1837 he won the Grand Prix de Rome. In 1847 he exhibited his enormous pseudo-classical Roman Orgy or Romans in the Decadence, now in the Luxembourg. This work gave a great impetus to the classic movement. Couture obtained many medals and the Legion of Honour; he decorated St. Eustache, Paris. Couture died 1879. He is represented at Boston and New York.
Description
A half length, standing, the body turned left, the head facing the spectator. She wears a white cap and dress, and holds a tabby cat; background grey-brown deepening to dark brown. Signed " T. Couture." A picture of Couture's less academic style, and one in closer touch with more modern tendencies. Couture is notorious for his attempted suppression of Marnet's development. Manet, the great influence of the XIXth Century and one of its permanent masters, studied under Couture, who stood in the ranks of the Classicists. Manet's tendency to revolt from semi-classic historical painting and to paint the world as he saw it disturbed Couture. But before such a picture as the above we might think it almost inexplicable that Couture should have found himself at variance with Manet's ambitions. Indeed, there is in this picture something of the Manet influence.
