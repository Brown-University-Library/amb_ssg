No. 50. MARRIAGE OF ST. CATHERINE
Artist note
The picture below has been attributed to the some- what vague, generic painter Bonifazio. At one time three Bonifazi were assumed; lately but one is permitted by the most expert authorities. The picture in question is in the nature of a pasticcio of Titian. The little St. John is derived from the Madonna with the Cherries at Vienna, the Madonna and St. Catherine from the Virgin and Child with Four Saints at Dresden. The Child, seated on His mother's hand and leaning forward, recalls Bonifazio's Adoration of the Magi in the Accademia, Venice.
Description
The Virgin is seated on the left, three-quarters length, three-quarters to the right; her head is bowed to the right. She wears a golden-brown hood, vermilion tunic, and deep mulberry cloak. On the left the boy St. John, seen only to the shoulders, looks up, profile right, holding out in his right hand a scroll inscribed " Ecce agnus." The Virgin holds with her two hands the little Christ, who leans forward in a sitting posture and places the ring on St. Catherine's right hand. She stands on the right profile left with bent head. Deep blue sky in the right and centre background; a dull red hanging on the left.
