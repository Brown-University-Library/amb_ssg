Artist note
Patrick Nasmyth, son of Alexander Nasmyth, was born in Edinburgh, and taught by his father. He went o London about 1807: from 1811 he exhibited at the Royal Academy. Having to a large extent lost the use of his right hand he had to learn to paint with his left. His best landscapes have a breadth and sense of atmosphere that his later works miss in reproducing an abundance of small detail. Nasmyth died in South Lambeth, 1831. He is well represented in the Edinburgh National Gallery and in the London Gallery. His work is not often as luminous and golden in tone as is the example described below.
Description
An English village scene. In the left and centre foreground, in shadow, a horse pond; children and a dog on the edge. Above them on the left red tiled houses built of stone and timber. Their fronts are in shadow, their sides in golden light. Before them in the road stand a man and woman looking up at a signpost, the man pointing to the right. The lane continues up the centre of the picture in cool shadow barred with sunlight, and branches off to the right behind a clump of trees, reappearing in the right mid-distance to lose itself between high hedges. Another cottage in the right mid-distance. A sky of delicate amber light and turquoise blue; warm grey summer clouds on the right. Signed "P. N."
