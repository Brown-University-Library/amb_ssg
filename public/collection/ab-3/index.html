Artist note
Beechey was born at Burford, Oxon. In 1772 he entered the Royal Academy Schools, and may have been taught there by Zoffany and Reynolds. He began to exhibit in 1776, thus being a competitor with Reynolds, Gainsborough, and Romney. From about 1783 until 1786 Beechey lived in Norwich. In 1793 he became an Associate of the Royal Academy (one year after Reynolds' death, five after Gainsborough's) and painter to the Queen. In 1798 he was elected a full member, and knighted. He continued to exhibit until his death in 1839. Portraits by Beechey are in the National Gallery, London, the Louvre, the National Portrait Gallery, London, and Kensington Palace. He is a good example of the portrait painters who revolved round Gainsborough and Reynolds. See W. Roberts: Sir William Beechey, R.A., 1907
Description
Bust portrait, the head turned three-quarters to the left. Scarlet uniform with black lapels, and gold chain braid from the right shoulder to the centre buttons. An elderly man, clean shaven, with grey hair. Dark sky background, yellow light low down on the left.
