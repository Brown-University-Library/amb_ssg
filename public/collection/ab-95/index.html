Description
A woman dressed in white, facing profile left, leaning back in a green wooden chair and seen at three-quarters left. Her left hand lies in her lap her right on an open book on a little table beside her. There, too, are a yellow rose and rose dore covering. Pale background. Signed " Louis Antoine Leclercq." Painted in the Whistler tradition; a study of " values," or gently contrasting tone and colour. In motif the works of this School are as different as possible from the picture by Kaemmerer, for example. While the latter primarily depends for its attractiveness on something approaching anecdote, or irrelevant interest such as the costume of such and such a period, the Impressionists of the Manet and Whistler School entirely rely upon qualities exclusively proper to painting, i.e. colour, tone relations, and the expression of unposed life. Thus they are nearer to the older painters such as Rembrandt and Velazquez, who always approached their subject from the standpoint of pictorial properties or conditions. Music affords the most lucid parallel. Music which attempted to introduce a picturesque or anecdotic element (such as bellringing, bird-whistling, clappers that imitate a horse's trotting, or the human voice) would be departing from the zone proper to music. It is thus with anecdotic painting.
