Artist note
Ibbetson was born in Yorkshire and apprenticed to a ship painter of Hull. He also painted scenery for "The Genii," a play performed in York and Hull. He went to London in 1777, there painting landscape, cattle and country people. He worked in obscurity till about 1785, when he began to exhibit. In 1788 Ibbetson was chosen as draughtsman to accompany Colonel Cathcart's embassy to China. On the voyage he gained some knowledge of ships and the sea, and immediately returned to London. His first wife's death in 1794, added to financial worries, undermined his health, and he retired to the country. He remarried in 1801. In 1803 he published An Accidence or Gamut of Painting in Oil (2nd edition, with a memoir, published 1828). He died 1817, at Masham, Yorkshire, his native place. After his death a volume of his etchings was published. Ibbetson, who is at his best a painter of considerable accomplishment and individual charm, is represented in the National Gallery and the Victoria and Albert Museum, London. His works are marked by a pleasant silvery atmosphere.
Description
In the right foreground a thatched cottage, cows and two calves. black and white, grouped round it, in late sunlight. On the extreme right a girl at the wash tub. Three cows on the left. Above them in the middle distance a large spired church; beyond are downs. The sky is a soft milky blue. Signed "Ibbetson, July 1807."
