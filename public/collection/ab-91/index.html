Artist note
Studied in Dusseldorf (1848-51). Painted The Old Kentucky Home (1867), The Old Stage Coach (1871), Milton dictating to his Daughters (1875). Eastman Johnson's portraits are well known.
Description
A farmer in a black boxer hat and bottle-green coat standing on the left raising his glass to the squire, who stands facing him on the right. Between them in the centre a Sheraton sideboard and a cellarette on the top, open, showing five decanters and the peacock-blue lining. A map inscribed "Nantucket " and a wax taper holder on the white wall to the left. Signed " E. Johnson, 1880." An intimate study of character and type. In conception and treatment this picture anticipates the recent English genre school by about twenty years. In subject this is a typical scene of New England life, in the middle of the XIXth Century. The squire was usually the judge or attorney who, living in a village, would purchase his wood and hay from his farmer neighbours. It was the custom in such cases to drink a glass, usually of " old Medford," or, as may be inferred from the glasses in this picture, of some more aristocratic wine. A Captain Baxter, an old shipowner, posed for Johnson frequently; he figures in Embers. It is he who is the squire in the above, and one John Fealey, an Irishman, is the farmer.
