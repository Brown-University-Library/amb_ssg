Artist note
West was born in Chester County, Pennsylvania. It seems that at eight years old he was taught by Cherokee Indians; later he moved to Philadelphia and New York, in both painting portraits. Making money thus, and assisted by good friends, he was able to go to Rome in 1760. He was a social success and remained in Italy three years, visiting Florence, Bologna, Venice, and Parma. In 1763 he arrived in London, where his reception was so promising that he sent home for his fiancee, and settled finally in England. He painted many pictures of sacred and profane history. His main contribution to art lies in his having painted English soldiers dressed in uniform instead of classical Roman costume. In 1768 he was one of four commissioners appointed to frame the constitution of the Royal Academy, and in 1772 was made historical painter to the King; he designed decorations for Windsor Castle. He succeeded Reynolds as P.R.A., but being a Quaker refused knighthood. West died in 1820 and was buried in St. Paul's. His Death of Wolfe is in Grosvenor House, London.
Description
Britannia is seated to the right, profile left. Behind her her shield, in her right hand a winged sceptre ending in an oar blade to denote naval dominion. Three maidens attendant by her, represent the arts of sculpture, architecture, and painting. In the centre putti receiving the riches of the East and Mediterranean that are laid at Britannia's feet. On the left stand an Indian chief, a Hindu woman, and a woman representing America, with hands outstretched. Behind her on the left a figure carrying a box. Sea with frigates in the centre distance. Signed " B. West, 1791." Exhibited at the Royal Academy in 1791 with a pendant sketch, " British Manufactory."
