No. 15. PORTRAIT OF A MAN
Artist note
The finest qualities of the minor German masters of the early XVIth Century are typified in the Portrait of a Man, possibly by Bartholomew Bruyn. Acquired at the last moment, this picture takes its proper place with the most important examples in the Memorial. (1913 Catalogue preface, p vi)
Description
Head and shoulders, seen against a pale blue sky and mountain distance. He wears a black velvet doublet, with a red silk lining, his hands are clasped before him. Hans Muelich and with more probability Bartholomaeus Bruyn have been suggested as the painters of this refined and masterly portrait. This period (early XVIth Century) of German Portraiture has not been satisfactorily explored. The name of Christopher Amberger is generally used, more or less generically, to cover numerous portraits whose common trait is an ascetic culture and refinement. Ludger to Ring is another master of this somewhat obscure period. His masterpiece perhaps is at present on loan in the Metropolitan Museum, New York. Another master is Barthel Beham. The picture in this Collection is among this class of sensitively refined and drawn portraits; others are in the Johnson Collection, Philadelphia.
