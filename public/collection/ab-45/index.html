Attributed to Steenwijck. No. 45. A SCENE FROM ROMAN HISTORY
Artist note
Born 1550, in Steenwijck, H. van Steenwijck the elder was pupil to J. de Vries, a painter of perspectives. Steenwijck followed him in this style. He was in especial fond of painting torchlight scenes. The Franckens usually introduced the figures in his pictures. Steenwijck was the master of P. Needs (see No. 33). He died about 1604. His pictures are in the Galleries at Amsterdam, Brussels, London, and Vienna. The picture below attributed to him somewhat resembles the picture in the National Gallery, London.
Description
The interior of a Renaissance palace, a marble- paved bed chamber with a green-canopied golden couch to the right, an aged king lying on it. At the foot of the couch a Roman in a toga stands with his back to the spectator addressing the king and pointing to the left background, where a centurion appears mounting a flight of stairs. The balustrade is of white stone with red marble pilasters; an orange tree in a bronze pot stands on the balus- trade; beyond a sphinx on a pedestal. An archway and distant door beyond. Sunlight falls on the background and across the foreground on the white and dull-rose marble flags. In the left foreground a Roman soldier carrying a tray with a small vessel on it. Above his head in the background a picture, apparently of a holy family. Signed " H. v. S . . . jk." The figures in this painting are clearly less delicate in handling, and are the work of a collaborator, possibly one of the Franckens.
