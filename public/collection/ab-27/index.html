Artist note
Born in Madrid presumably about 1600, del Mazo entered Velazquez' studio when quite young and remained until the master's death (1660). In 1634 he married Velazquez' daughter, and in 1660 succeeded to his office as Court painter. He died in 1667. From the few signed examples of his work that are known he can be put down as a skilled reproducer of the external qualities of Velazquez' painting. In the inner properties, such as crisp unerring draughtsmanship, peculiar beauty of texture, intimate and unreproducible mastery of pigment, and above all in human understanding, del Mazo does not pretend to compete with his master. Yet such was his training and, we must recognise, his adaptability that were no single example of Velazquez' mature work extant del Mazo would take rank as an unusual master. The portraits such as the Innocent X in this Collection, usually assigned to him, are inevitably judged by a superlatively exacting standard; and in that comparison del Mazo suffers. But if, for example, the Doria Palace and Hermitage portraits of Innocent were destroyed we should be able from del Mazo's copies to form a better idea of Velazquez than we could in similar circumstances form of any other painter. As things are, however, "every portrait by del Mazo, in the Prado and elsewhere, is stamped as a secondhand Velazquez." His best picture is generally said to be the Don Tiburcio y Cruzat in the Prado; he also painted landscape and sea pieces. See C. G. Hartley: Spanish Painting. 1904.
