Artist note
Born in Ghent, trained under Gerome in Paris. Awarded a third class Medal in the Paris Salon 1874. He lived in Paris. His death by suicide occurred in 1892. Among his pictures, all of which dealt with costume genre, are Incroyables (1870), Vanderbilt Coll., New York; Une Dispute (1872), W. Rockefeller Coll.; Coast of Scheveningen (1875), Corcoran Coll., Baltimore; Une Marriage sous le Directoire and Automme dans les Tuileries.
Description
A scene in the Tuileries or Luxembourg Gardens in the Directoire. A flight of steps across the foreground. At the top, about to descend, a lady in white, a fan in her left hand, looking to the right, where stands a dandy, holding a cane and looking round at une Merveilleuse. Behind, to left and right, groups promenading under the trees or seated criticising. Two sparrows flying down the steps. Signed " F. H. Kaemmerer." Dutch genre of the XIXth century dealing with the interesting period and costume of the French Directoire and Empire. Kaemmerer, who is represented in American Collections and in the Stedelijk Museum, Amsterdam, had a considerable vogue. His art to some extent corresponds with the work produced in England by the late followers of the Pre-Raphaelite movement, such as Marcus Stone, R.A. The above picture is alluded to in Bryan's Dictionary- Merveilleuses sous le Directoire and Une Dispute (now in Mr. W. Rockefeller's Collection) " are good examples of his style; indeed it was they who (sic) definitely established his reputation."
