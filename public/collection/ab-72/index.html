Artist note
Casado, a man of patrician family, was born in Valencia; he studied in San Fernandos and Madrid. In 1860 he won the Prix de Rome, and first class medals in Madrid in 1862-64 and 1881. He was Director of the Spanish Academy and Court painter. He died 1895. He had the Cross of the Order of Isabel, given for his Bell of Huesca, now in the Modern Museum, Madrid.
Description
The interior of the harem. The favourite, a white woman, reclines in the centre, naked to the waist. Round her waist a chalk-white drapery, contrasting with the rose-pearl flesh and the deep sea-blue robe covering her legs. Her left arm extended behind her on a turquoise-green silk shawl that contrasts with the rose-grey flesh and gold background. Behind the figure on the left a bronze-amber coloured hanging; to he right white marble. Beneath the body and in the foreground deep mulberry colour, light indigo-blue and crimson. In the extreme left foreground is repeated the turquiose-green of the silk shawl. Flowers in a pot, right, in the background. Signed " Casado." A Spanish writer has mentioned this picture as " One of his latest works is one of the favourites of fiction; ' belle et nonchalante,' dirait le poete, ' inutile et dangereuse,' repondrait le philosophe." The picture is a study in the emotional value of colour, sweet and delicate and sensuous, like music of this kind. Other pictures by Casado are The Death of Ferdinand IV, Semiramis, Goya's Studio, Beheaded Arabs, In the Boudoir (1882), Temptation (1884), and Laura (1885).
