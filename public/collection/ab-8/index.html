Artist note
Claudio Coello, not to be confused with Alonzo or Juan de Coello, was born in Madrid at a date not ascertained. He studied in Italy under F. Rizi, and by copying the Venetian masters and Rubens. He was one of Philip IV's painters, employed in the Escorial. His works, which are said to combine certain properties of Alonzo Cano, Murillo, and Velazquez, may be seen in convents in Salamanca, Saragoza, and Madrid. His chief work is the altar piece in the Sacristy of S. Lorenzo, in the Escorial. Coello, to whom the portrait below is assigned, died in 1693.
Description
Almost a full length, full face. Her hair is black and dressed high on the head, with a long plaited tress hanging over the right shoulder in front. She wears a rose-red dress trimmed with pearls and an underskirt of grey silk embroidered with various flowers. A close-fitting ostrich feather head-dress set with single beads of jet covers the hair. Behind her a dark red curtain on the left. In type the sitter is not unlike Maria d'Este, the second wife of James II of England. A favourite painter of hers was Benedetto Gennari, to whose work this portrait bears certain resemblances. This Gennari worked in Italy and in England. A signed example of his portraiture is in the Goodwood Collection, England.
