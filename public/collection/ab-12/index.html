No. 12. THE PHILOSOPHER, or ST. JEROME IN HIS STUDY
Artist note
This type of picture originating in Quinten Massys (1466-1530) represents the birth of the early XVIth Century genre in the Netherlands, as opposed to religioius paintings. Beginning with these comparatively serious themes - philosophers sitting in secluded contemplation of our inevitable lot while the world passes on its careless way outside, Flemish and Dutch genre quickly introduced a more amusing kind of subject, culminating in the XVIIth or early XVIIIth Century version of the Reymerswael and Massys type of philopsphers. There are many versions of it, one in the Jhnson Collection, Philadelphia. One is known bearing the monogram of Jan Massys.
Description
An elderly grey bearded man, in a deep vermilion robe, seated behind a table, his head resting on his right hand, his left forefinger on the temple of a skull. Before him on the left his reading desk with an open book thereon, illuminated with an Ascension. On a lower shelf of the desk two books, one inscribed MCCCVI, another PAULI XV. On the left a crucifix, a window with round leaded glass, and an iris in a pot. Towards the right on the table a pair of snuffers, a guttered candle in a brass stick, a scarlet hour glass, and four volumes. An open window in the background gives on to a distant village with figures and horses and distant rocky hills. Formerly attributed to Quinten Massys. NOTE TO No. 12. This painting was purchased from Martin Colnaghi, who, during the better part of his life, had made a special study of the works of the early Low Countries painters, and was regarded as an authority whose opinion was worthy of consideration. He sold this for a work by Quinten Massys, and in positive terms to the writer confirmed this statement, which since has been sustained by several well-known experts. R.C.H.
