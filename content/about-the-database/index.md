## Genesis of the project

Suzanne Karr ’01 recalled:

> I conceived the idea of [for this project] early in the fall semester while discussing the collection’s past with the memorial’s caretaker, Carole Cramer. Subsequently I invited Edward Wouk ’02, a fellow art history student, to join on. He in turn brought Miguel Santos-Neves ’01 on board to fill the role of adjunct historian ... I have loved the Annmary Brown Memorial throughout my tenure at Brown and believe many more students should remember it so fondly.

## Implementation

The initiative and enthusiasm of these three Brown University undergraduates, who discovered and wanted to share more widely the hidden delights of the Hawkins art collection, were the driving force in getting this project off the ground.

In developing their ideas for how best to accomplish their goals, Suzanne, Ed, and Miguel consulted with many Brown University faculty and staff, among them Jo-Ann Conklin, Director of the Bell Gallery; Norine Duncan, Curator of the Art Slide Library; Dian Kriz, Associate Professor of History of Art & Architecture; Carole Cramer, Administrator of the Annmary Brown Memorial; Samuel Streit, Associate University Librarian for Special Collections; and Jean Rainwater, Coordinator of Reader Services.

After many preliminary conversations, Suzanne and Miguel successfully applied for an Undergraduate Teaching and Research Assistantship (UTRA) grant to work with library staff to create a database of the works of art in the Annmary Brown Memorial Collection during the Spring 2001 semester. Dian Kriz was the faculty sponsor for this project. Ed, who spent the semester abroad, worked further on the collection as a senior thesis project under the direction of Professor Kermit Champa.

## Methodology

The database for this collection was created in FileMaker Pro 4.1. Suzanne and Miguel created database fields that reflect standard graphics-cataloging practice, entered data from the collection files, added references and notes, and verified the location of all items.

They also included the full text of a catalog of the collection published in 1913. This catalog was scanned using optical character-recognition software and proofread by Reader Services student assistant Melanie Knapp ’03. Most of the black-and-white reproductions were scanned by Reader Services student Christopher Raymond ’03, and much of the color photography was shot by Ravi Sitwala ’01.

Jean Rainwater edited the database to optimize it for web searching, incorporated the images, designed the original website, and wrote the introduction to the collection. The database and images were mounted on the Library’s Windows NT server by Joseph Mancino, Senior Library Associate Specialist, Systems.

Internal administrative and appraisal data are not published.

## Organization and contents of the FileMaker Pro database

- **Item ID.** This number, beginning with the prefix “AB,” corresponds to the numbering in the 1913 catalog, which has 111 numbered entries plus an addenda of six items in the second edition. The collection files are organized by this number.

- **Image.** Most of the collection files contain black-and-white 8-by-10-inch prints of the paintings, which were scanned at 150 dpi. A contact roll of thumbnail prints, containing images of some paintings for which no file could be found, was scanned at 300 dpi. Color images were made either by scanning a color transparency or shooting with a Nikon Coolpix 800 digital camera. The images in the original site were resized to 200 pixels.

- **Artist.** This field contains a list of the artists as identified by Christie’s in 1984. These attributions do not agree in all cases with the 1913 catalog, for which a separate field was established.

- **Artist dates.** Data was entered from the collection files or other reference sources.

- **Time period.** Because the date field mixes dates—for example, 1750–1800—and text—for example, nineteenth century—this field was established to allow broad chronological searching.

- **Nationality.** Data was entered from the collection files or other reference sources.

- **Title.** The title was entered from the 1913 catalog or the collection files.

- **Artwork date.** Most of the works are not dated with any certainty. Dates were entered when they were included in the 1913 catalog or collection files.

- **Provenance.** Exact provenance information is not available for many of the paintings. Where it appears in the 1913 catalog or the collection files, it is included. Further research in Hawkins’ correspondence and other sources is needed.

- **Acquisition date.** In many cases it is only possible to give the date before which the painting must have been acquired. Further research in Hawkins’ correspondence and other sources is needed.

- **Medium.** Data was entered from the 1913 catalog or collection files.

- **Dimensions.** Data was entered from the 1913 catalog or collection files.

- **Location.** Most of the paintings were located in the Annmary Brown Memorial; a few of the paintings and all of the prints were in the John Hay Library when the database was assembled.

- **Room.** The location within each building.

- **1913 catalog entry.** This field makes it possible to distinguish records according to whether they were entered in the 1913 catalog and explains why the 1913 catalog fields are blank in some records.

- **1913 catalog artist.** The artist as identified by the cataloger C. H. Collins Baker.

- **1913 catalog dates.** The dates as identified by C. H. Collins Baker.

- **1913 catalog school.** The school as identified by C. H. Collins Baker.

- **1913 catalog note.** C. H. Collins Baker’s notes on the artist, with occasional notes signed by Hawkins and identified by “R.C.H.” in the catalog.

- **1913 catalog title.** The title or subject as identified by C. H. Collins Baker.

- **1913 catalog description.** A description of the painting by C. H. Collins Baker, with occasional notes signed by Hawkins and identified by “R.C.H.” in the catalog.

- **References.** References from the 1913 catalog, collection files, or other sources.

- **BU inventory number.** The Brown University inventory number was added from the collection files. If an item had no file, the project did not attempt to find the inventory label on the painting itself. These inventory numbers reflect a system no longer in current use.
