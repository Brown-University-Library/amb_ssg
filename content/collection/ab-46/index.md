catalog_note = "Stuart was born in Narraganset, Rhode Island. He was taught by a Scottish painter-Alexander, of Newport, who took him to Scotland. Returning to America, he settled in Boston and then New York. Finding, however, imperfect encouragement Stuart in 1778 set sail for England. After a period of desultory occupations he became Benjamin West's pupil in 1780.  At length he began to practise independently. In 1786 he married, and about 1788 went to Dublin, where he had great success. In 1793 he returned to America, stopping in New York and going on to Philadelphia, where he painted Washington. Portraits and repetitions of \" the Father of Freedom \" by and after Stuart abound. In a general way they do not represent his best work. Stuart moved subsequently to Washington and in 1806 to Boston, where he died in 1828. He was a brilliant conversationalist and skilled musician. As a portrait painter he atones for technical deficiencies with a curiously individual way of seeing his sitters. He is represented in Boston, Washington, New York, the National Portrait Gallery (where some of his best work is) and National Gallery, London, and at Yale University."
