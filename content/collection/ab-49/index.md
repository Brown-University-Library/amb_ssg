catalog_note = "John Trumbull, born in Lebanon 1756, entered the Army in 1775. He seems to have had a firm liking for drawing from his earliest days. In 1777, resigning his commission, he returned to Boston and to painting.  He took Smibert's painting room and copied some of his works; but finding no one in Boston capable of helping him, and yet hankering after a military life, he volunteered as an aide to General Sullivan, with whom he served in the Rhode Island campaign of 1778. Then he returned to Boston, and in 1780 sailed for Europe. By way of Paris he went to England. He was arrested after Major Andre's death, but well treated. Through Benjamin West's aid he was at length freed; he had meanwhile copied a Correggio. Returning to America Trumbull made up his mind to take up painting as a profession and again sailed for London, arriving 1784. His pictures  appeared from 1786 (Bunker's Hill). Back in America in 1789 Trumbull stayed here till 1794, then going on diplomatic work to England. There he painted during 1808-1815. In 1817 he was decorating the Capitol, Washington. He was president of the Academy, New York. Most of Trumbull's pictures are at Yale. He died 1843."

The hair is powdered grey, the chin  slightly raised. Four buttons seen on his coat. Grey background. Signed \" 1776, J. T.\"\n\n In Trumbull's Autobiography a list is given of pictures painted before 1780, before the painter had had any instruction. On this list occurs a portrait of General Washington; done from memory of another picture by Peale. He held an exhibition in New York before he left for England; No. 57 in the catalogue was \" Washington, half length, from memory.\" The picture described above has been traditionally identified with this No. 57, which, after the sale of 1780, was lost sight of. An engraving by Blanchard- \"dessine par Conder \" -resembles this portrait.\n\n A label, said to be inscribed in Trumbull's writing and formerly affixed to this portrait, bears this legend: \"Life-size bust portrait of Gen. Washington in 1776. Exhib. in N. York and admired very much. taken by me to England, had to hide it away with my friend)  B.W.-J.T.\""
