catalog_note = "Copley was born of Anglo-Irish parents in Boston, U.S.A. He won a reputation in his native city as a portrait painter before 1774, when he left for Italy en route for England. In London in 1776 he became an Associate of the Royal Academy; in 1779 he became a full member. Before 1783 he painted his Death of Chatham and Death of Major Pierson, the former in the House of Lords, London, the latter in the National Gallery, London. In the Death of Major Pierson Copley is seen at his best- a master of pigment, an able designer, and able to express without exaggeration a certain dramatic convention. He sent  forty-three pictures to the Royal Academy, and eight to the British Institution. Copley is well represented as a portrait painter at Boston, Washington, and in the Metropolitan Museum, New York. He died in London, September 9, 1815"

description = "Half length standing, nearly full face, in scarlet and ermine robes. His left arm draped with heavy folds of white silk and ermine is akimbo, against the sky. The right is extended right across the canvas to the left, the hand hanging over the edge of a rock. A pillar on the left background and one in the centre, behind the head; to left and right, beyond, tree stems and foliage against a sky of singularly rich egg-blue. An orange glow low down on the right.\n\n Formerly in St. George's Hall, Easton-in-Gordano, N. Somersetshire, England. Though traditionally attributed to Copley, this portrait does not satisfactorily tally with his style. It is clearly influenced by Reynolds, and is nearer Hoppner than Copley. It was formerly called The Earl of Chatham, for whom it is much too young."
