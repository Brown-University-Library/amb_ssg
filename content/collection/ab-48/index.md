catalog_note = "Tiepolo, one of the last great Venetian masters, was born in Venice, and studied under Lazzarini. The influence of Paolo Veronese and of that brilliant virtuoso, Piazzetta, are traceable in him. He married Guardi's sister in 1715. Till about 1740 Tiepolo was busy in and round Venice; he decorated the churches and the great palazza and villas. In 1750 he went to Wurtzburg to adorn the Archbishop's palace, returning to Venice in 1753. In 1755 he was elected Director of the Venice Academy; in 1762 he resigned and went at the Spanish king's invitation to Madrid to decorate the Royal Palace. His reception was magnificent, and his hands were filled with commissions. He died in Madrid, 1770. Venetian art may be said to have come to a brilliant end with Tiepolo and Guardi. The fervent piety of the Venetian School under Bellini had died out two hundred years before Tiepolo. In its place the sumptuous exuberance of colour, design and physical well-being initiated by Giorgione had run for two centuries and a half, expressed by Titian, Palma, Tintorett, Paolo Veronese, and lastly Tiepolo. He summed up and crystallised, in terms of his own day, the essence of the late Renaissance in Venice.\n\n See Fransz Hermann Meissner: Tietolo. Leipzig, 1897. E.
