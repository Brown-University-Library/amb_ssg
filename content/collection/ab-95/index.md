In motif the works of this School are as different as possible from the picture by Kaemmerer, for example. While the latter primarily depends for its attractiveness on something approaching anecdote, or irrelevant interest such as the costume of such and such a period, the Impressionists of the Manet and Whistler School entirely rely upon qualities exclusively proper to painting, i.e. colour, tone relations, and the expression of unposed life. Thus they are nearer to the older painters such as Rembrandt and Velazquez, who always approached their subject from the standpoint of pictorial properties or conditions.  Music affords the most lucid parallel.
