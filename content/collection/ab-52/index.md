catalog_note = "West was born in Chester County, Pennsylvania. It seems that at eight years old he was taught by Cherokee Indians; later he moved to Philadelphia and New York, in both painting portraits. Making money thus, and assisted by good friends, he was able to go to Rome in 1760. He was a social success and remained in Italy three years, visiting Florence, Bologna, Venice, and Parma. In 1763 he arrived in London, where his reception was so promising that he sent home for his fiancee, and settled finally in England.  He painted many pictures of sacred and profane history. His main contribution to art lies in his having painted English soldiers dressed in uniform instead of classical Roman costume. In 1768 he was\n one of four commissioners appointed to frame the constitution of the Royal Academy, and in 1772 was made historical painter to the King; he designed decorations for Windsor Castle. He succeeded Reynolds as P.R.A., but being a Quaker refused knighthood. West died in 1820 and was buried in St. Paul's. His Death of Wolfe is in Grosvenor House, London."
