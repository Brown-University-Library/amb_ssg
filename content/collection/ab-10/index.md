catalog_note = "Michael Dahl was a Swedish portrait painter, trained in France and Italy, and practising in England. Born in 1659 he came to England soon after Lely's death (1680); then went to France and Rome, returning to England in 1688. He quickly gained a reputation and became a serious rival to Sir Godfrey Kneller. He painted Queen Anne and Prince George of Denmark, and had a large practice among the aristocratic class.  His masterpiece is his Sir Cloudesley Shovell, the famous admiral, painted about 1702, and now in the Painted Hall, Greenwich, England. His Queen Anne as Princess is in the National Portrait Gallery, London, where too are other pieces by him. At his best Dahl was a capable and accomplished painter. Living to a great age (d. 1743) he produced inferior work after 1735.\n\n See C. H. Collins Baker: Lely and the Stuart Portrait Painters. London. 1912"
