catalog_note = "Born at Bristol Lawrence was by 1780 working as a pastel portrait painter at Oxford. Then he set up in Bath, and in 1787 moved to London, and entered the Royal Academy Schools. In 1791, too young to be a regular Associate of the Academy, he was made a Supplemental Associate. Reynolds dying in 1792, Lawrence succeeded him as painter in ordinary to the King. In 1794 he was a Royal Academician. Between 1787 and 1830 he sent 311 pictures to the Academy. His best pictures, such as Miss Farren (Pierpont Morgan Collection) and F. Angerstein and his Wife (Louvre), and the Bathurst portraits are brilliantly attractive. His pencil drawings are remarkable for honesty of handling and sympathy of interpretation. But Lawrence succeeded too well; he was the typical popular Academician, who threw off commissions, in which he felt no interest, with no effort and at no cost. In consequence he was knighted and medalled to the top of his bent. As a collector of masters' drawings Lawrence is known to have shown fine taste; his collection was unequalled. He is about the last of the prominent painters who in the great private English galleries carried on the line of portraits started in Elizabeth's reign. With Lawrence this line breaks, and is only intermittently taken up by isolated portraits; photographs fill in the gaps. Lawrence, who succeeded West as P.R.A. in 1820, died in 1830."
