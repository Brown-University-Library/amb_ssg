catalog_note = "Born near Naples and at first taught by his father, Angelo Solimena, Francesco was sent to Naples in 1674 to work under Francesco di Maria. From him he passed to Giacomo del Po. Later he went to Rome, emulating there the works of P. da Cortona, Lanfranco and Guido. In time Solimena became himself a model to the rising generation. He, as well as P. da Cortona, Guido, and the rest, was the admired objective of the French students who in the XVIIIth Century were sent to Rome to acquire the grand style. Solimena's best works are at Naples; others are in Dresden, Milan, Paris and Rome. In 1702 he was commissioned by Philip V of Spain to paint pictures for the Royal Chapel, Madrid. Dying in Naples, 1747, at the age of ninety, Solimena bequeathed a large school of imitators. To Solimena, Sebastiano Conca and the other Neapolitans is due the especial significance now attached to the term Neapolitan. By the word we understand a school of chiaroscurists who, while conventional and mannered and almost wholly divorced from real life, were yet considerable masters of the brush. The example described below is in this way typical of the Neapolitan XVIIth and XVIIIth Century School."
